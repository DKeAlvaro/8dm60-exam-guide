    4 · Models for hospital data#

    Subtitles#

    1. Static, longitudinal, and multimodal patient representations
    2. Information loss in EHR representations
    3. Prevalence, discrimination, and precision
    4. Labels, treatment, and selection bias
    5. Temporal leakage and live-use evaluation
    6. Calibration and expected outcomes
    7. Dataset shift, missingness, and fairness

    Milestones#

    • Find what an aggregation or representation throws away.
    • Move sensitivity, specificity, and precision across prevalence.
    • Diagnose treatment, label, missingness, and leakage bias.
    • Separate AUROC, calibration, and observed/expected ratios.
    • Design patient-level, temporal, external, and subgroup tests.

    Possible exam questions#

    OPEN · Q1 — What is lost when vital-sign sequences become stay-level means and note chunks become one averaged embedding? Propose fixes and their costs.#

    OPEN · Q2 — A balanced test gives sensitivity = specificity = precision = 0.70. At 10% prevalence, which metrics transfer and what is the approximate precision?#

    OPEN · Q3 — How do treated septic-shock negatives bias risk estimates? What happens if treated patients are removed from training or evaluation?#

    OPEN · Q4 — Why can a bidirectional LSTM work at hour 24 but fail for hourly alerts? How should retrospective training and evaluation mimic live use?#

    OPEN · Q5 — Model A: AUROC 0.91, mean risk 19%; B: 0.88, 8.5%; true mortality 8%. Which is calibrated, and what O/E ratio follows for 200 patients?#

    OPEN · Q6 — Does higher AUROC in an X-ray subgroup prove multimodal benefit for everyone? What changes at a hospital with different modality availability?#

    OPEN · Q7 — False-positive rates rise with age. Will removing age fix the disparity? Propose an alternative and its audit measurements.#
